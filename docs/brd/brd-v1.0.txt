1. Executive summary
1.1 The problem
Many dine-in restaurants still run service on paper order slips (KOTs), verbal hand-offs and
waiters walking back and forth between the floor, the kitchen and the cash counter. This
causes:
slow and error-prone order transfer to the kitchen;
food waiting at the pass because the right waiter doesn't know it is ready;
missed or slow responses to diners who want water, a waiter or the bill;
little systematic upselling;
weak visibility for owners into who did what, including revenue leakage through deleted
or edited bills and items sent to the kitchen but never billed.
1.2 The solution
A locally hosted, integrated restaurant operations platform that digitises the full order
cycle, from the diner's table to the kitchen and back to the bill. It has these components:

Core design principle: local-first. Everything that happens inside the restaurant (ordering,
kitchen, pagers, billing, reports) works without internet. The cloud is used only for what
must cross the internet: QR orders from diners' phones, licensing, updates, off-site backups
and vendor monitoring.
1.3 Business value
Faster service. Orders reach the correct kitchen station instantly; the right waiter is
paged the moment food is ready or a table needs attention.
Higher average bill. Recommendations on the tablet, QR menu and waiter app.
Control and trust. Every action is attributed to a person and device. Bills can't be
deleted, voids and discounts need approval, and a daily suspicious-activity report goes to
the owner.
Low running cost for the restaurant. Data stays on its own PC; the kitchen display runs
on inexpensive Android hardware.
Recurring revenue for the vendor. Subscription model with remote license control and
remote updates.

6. Functional requirements
Each requirement is written as ID (priority), followed by the requirement. Items marked "
(blueprint)" come from the original development blueprint; everything else was added
during the requirements workshop.
6.1 Authentication, sessions and device pairing (AUTH)
AUTH-001 (M) Staff log in on shared devices by selecting their name/photo tile, then
entering a numeric PIN (4 digits by default; the owner may require 6 ⚙ ). Choosing a
name first means PINs need not be unique, and setting a PIN can never reveal someone
else's.
AUTH-002 (M) PINs shall never be stored, transmitted in logs or displayed in plain text.
They shall be hashed with bcrypt (cost ≥ 12) or Argon2id (blueprint). Before hashing,
they are combined with a server-side secret ("pepper") kept outside the database and
protected by Windows DPAPI. A 4-digit PIN has only 10,000 combinations, so without the
pepper a stolen database could be brute-forced in seconds.
AUTH-003 (M) After 5 failed attempts within 10 minutes (blueprint) a staff login is
locked for 15 minutes ⚙  or until a manager unlocks it. Attempts are also rate-limited per
device.
AUTH-004 (M) One reusable PIN-pad component is used for login and manager overrides
across the POS, KDS, waiter app and table tablet (blueprint).
AUTH-005 (M) On successful login the server issues a short-lived signed access token (≤
15 min ⚙ ) bound to staff member, role, restaurant and device, plus a refresh mechanism
(blueprint: JWT).
Sessions expire after inactivity (POS and waiter devices: 10 min ⚙ ) and are renewed
automatically while the person is actively using the app (blueprint).
KDS screens run in device-authenticated station mode: actions are attributed to the
station. Individual kitchen logins can be switched on ⚙ .
=== PAGE 18 ===
AUTH-006 (M) The Owner account uses a strong password (≥ 12 characters) plus a TOTP
second factor, which works offline. Both are required for: archive and purge, backup
restore, full data export, license/subscription screens, tax and invoice settings, and
creating or removing managers.
AUTH-007 (M) Device pairing. Every device (KDS screen, waiter phone, table tablet,
pager, manager's browser) must be paired by a manager before it can talk to the server.
1. The manager generates a one-time pairing QR code (valid 10 min ⚙ ).
2. The device presents it.
3. The server issues a per-device credential (key pair or client certificate), stored in the
device's secure storage (Android Keystore, ESP32 encrypted storage, browser non-
exportable key).
Requests from unpaired devices are rejected even when a valid PIN is entered.
AUTH-008 (M) Unpairing a device, or deactivating a staff member, revokes their tokens
and closes their live connections within 5 seconds.
AUTH-009 (M) Each table tablet is paired to one table; re-assigning it requires a manager
PIN. A tablet can only read or act on its own table.
AUTH-010 (M) Role-based access control per §4.2 is enforced on the server for every
REST call, socket event and MQTT topic. Hiding buttons in the UI is not a security control.
AUTH-011 (M) Manager override: actions marked PIN in §4.2 prompt for a manager's PIN
on the current device. The audit log records both the requester and the approver.
AUTH-012 (S) The Owner can create custom roles by combining permissions.
AUTH-013 (M) Logins, logouts, failed attempts, lockouts and overrides are written to the
audit log (AUD).
AUTH-014 (M) Vendor staff have no standing access to a restaurant's local system (see
UPD-008, UPD-009).

ONB-002 (M) Data drive selection. The installer lists local fixed drives with free space
and defaults to the first non-system drive (e.g., D:) if one exists. Otherwise it uses C: and

ONB-003 (M) Activation requires a one-time activation code issued from the Vendor
Control Plane. It binds the license to the restaurant and the PC (LIC-004).
ONB-004 (M) The first-run setup wizard is usable by a non-technical manager, saves

1. Restaurant profile: legal name, display name, address, state, GSTIN, FSSAI license

restaurant, never hard-coded), SAC code, prices tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive ⚙ ,
rounding rule.
3. Invoice settings: series prefix, financial-year reset, header/footer, service charge (off
by default).
4. Accounts: Owner (password + 2FA), managers.
5. Floor: sections and tables (number/name, capacity). Visual drag-and-drop layout is S.
6. Stations and printers: kitchen stations; network printer auto-discovery (S) or manual
IP (blueprint); test print; station ↔ printer mapping; per-station mode screen / print /
both (blueprint).
7. Menu import (ONB-005, ONB-006).
8. Staff and PINs, section assignment.
9. Device pairing: tablets, waiter phones, KDS screens, pagers.
10. QR codes: generate and print table tents (PDF, A6/A5).
11. Go-live check: a guided test order through every path (waiter, tablet, QR, KDS, pager,
bill) before going live.
ONB-005 (M) Excel/CSV menu import using a vendor-provided template: categories,
items, prices, tax group, veg/non-veg/egg, tags, station, description, synonyms. Variants,
modifiers and combos go in companion sheets. A validation report lists every error before
anything is committed.
ONB-006 (S) AI-assisted menu import. Upload photos or a PDF of the printed menu. A
Vendor Control Plane service extracts it into the template structure. A mandatory human

review screen shows every item before commit. Requires internet; the AI provider's API

ONB-007 (S) Bulk photo upload, matched automatically to items by file name or item

6.11 Recommendation engine (REC)
REC-001 (M) A three-layer engine runs inside the local server. Results are served to the
tablet and waiter app, and published as a snapshot to the QR relay.
REC-002 (M) Layer 1, manual rules (highest priority). The restaurant defines rules such
as "if the order contains item/category X, suggest item/category Y", each with a priority,
an optional time-of-day window, an optional date range and target channels. Rules are
managed on the dashboard.
REC-003 (M) Layer 2, learned "frequently ordered together" (machine learning).
Association-rule mining (e.g., FP-Growth/Apriori, or co-occurrence with lift) over that
restaurant's own orders in a rolling window (90 days ⚙ ).
Retrained nightly on the local server; no cloud required.
Minimum support, confidence and lift thresholds ⚙ .
Activated only after a minimum order volume (300 orders ⚙ ) to avoid noisy
suggestions.
REC-004 (M) Layer 3, best sellers. Used when nothing has been ordered yet or no rule
applies. Ranked by quantity over the last 30 days ⚙  and segmented by time of day
(breakfast / lunch / evening / dinner windows ⚙ ).
REC-005 (M) Filters applied to all layers:
exclude out-of-stock items;
exclude items already in the order, unless the item is marked "repeatable" (e.g.,
beverages, breads) ⚙ ;
respect the table's veg-only filter;
course awareness: favour the next course in a configurable category sequence
(starters → mains → breads → desserts → beverages ⚙ ).
REC-006 (M) Every recommendation carries a human-readable reason label.
=== PAGE 32 ===
REC-007 (M) Recommendations are table-level only: no personal profiles and no cross-
visit history in v1.
REC-008 (M) Tracking per layer and per item: impressions, taps, add-to-cart, ordered.
This feeds the recommendation-performance report (RPT-012).
REC-009 (S) The manager can boost or pin items (e.g., high margin, overstocked) and
block items from being recommended.
REC-010 (C) Personalised recommendations with the diner's consent; A/B testing of
strategies.
REC-011 (M) Recommendations are returned in ≤ 200 ms on the LAN.

per-installation private certificate authority, trusted and pinned in the vendor's own
apps and installed on paired manager browsers; or (b) publicly trusted certificates for
a per-restaurant hostname resolving to the LAN IP, issued via DNS validation by the
Control Plane.
SEC-002 (M) Secrets are never in source code or in app bundles. API keys for cloud
services live only in vendor cloud secret stores and CI. Restaurant installations get per-
tenant, scoped credentials from the Control Plane. Clients get short-lived tokens.
SEC-003 (M) Authorisation: deny by default, checked on the server, including object-
level checks (a tablet may act only on its own table, a waiter only on permitted tables, a
pager only on its own topic).
SEC-004 (M) Input handling:
validation at every boundary with shared Zod schemas; output encoding;
parameterised queries only (Prisma), with no string-built SQL;

test):
Row-Level Security on every relay/Control Plane table, deny by default.
Local servers use per-tenant credentials, never a service-role key.
Browsers never write tables directly (QR-006).
Automated cross-tenant access tests run in CI.

Each local database holds only one restaurant, but still stores the restaurant ID on every

Code-signing keys are on hardware tokens or cloud HSM.

9.4 Data rules for developers
Money is stored as integer paise, never as floating-point numbers. Rounding rules are
explicit and tested.
Timestamps are stored in UTC and shown in IST. Every business record also carries its
business date, because night service after midnight belongs to the previous day until the
cut-off time.
Soft-delete/archive for master data; no hard deletes for transactional data (AUD-004).
Referential integrity is enforced in the database. Every migration is reviewed and
reversible by restore (UPD-005).
All cloud data is stored and processed in India.

10. Technical architecture and recommended stack
10.1 Architecture principles
1. Local-first: the local server is the single source of truth for the restaurant. The cloud is an
extension, never a dependency for service.
=== PAGE 56 ===
2. Modular, ports and adapters (INT): isolated modules, contract-first, integration-ready.
3. Event-driven real time: state changes emit domain events. Events reach apps via
Socket.io and pagers via MQTT. Cross-boundary delivery uses an outbox/inbox with
idempotency keys.
4. Secure by default (§8).
5. Everything versioned: APIs, events, menus, licenses, firmware, database schema.
6. One UI codebase per platform family: web (POS, dashboard, KDS) and React Native
(waiter app, tablet), sharing design tokens and domain logic.

18. Testing and acceptance
18.1 Test levels
Unit (domain logic), integration (server modules and database), contract (INT-002), end-to-
end (Playwright for web/desktop, Maestro for Android), firmware hardware-in-the-loop,
load/performance (§7.2), security (§8), usability (§7.4), and the pilot.
18.2 Critical scenarios (all must pass before the pilot)
ID Scenario
S1 Dinner rush: 40 tables, 3 stations, 8 waiters, 25 tablets, 10 pagers, 600 orders in 3 hours
(simulated + real devices). All §7.1 targets met; zero lost or duplicate orders/KOTs.
S2 Internet outage for 30 min mid-service: in-restaurant flows unaffected; QR read-only;
queues drain after recovery with no duplicates.
S3 Server restart mid-service: clients reconnect ≤ 30 s; no lost orders or alerts.
S4 Printer failure: alert raised, KOTs queued/redirected, nothing lost.
S5 Pager out of range / battery dead: alerts reach the waiter app; if both are unavailable,
the manager is alerted immediately.
S6 Tablet session: open → order → approval (app and at-table PIN) → status →
Water/Cancel → bill → reset; nothing carried over.
S7 Move table with tickets in progress: tickets updated in place, not duplicated.
S8 License: overdue → grace → restricted (only at next business day) → reactivation
online and offline; data export works in restricted mode.
S9 Update failure: forced failed health check → automatic rollback; restaurant keeps
working.
S10 Backup and restore to a new PC; archive-then-purge with verification; archive viewer.
S11 Cross-tenant isolation in the relay and Control Plane (automated).
S12 Fraud patterns: void after print, repeated reprints, over-limit discount, KOT never
billed. All appear in reports with the correct people attributed.

18.3 Acceptance
Every M requirement is linked to at least one passing test, in a traceability matrix
maintained by the team.
Defect severity: Sev-1 service stopped or data lost/incorrect money; Sev-2 major function
impaired, no workaround; Sev-3 workaround exists; Sev-4 cosmetic.
Pilot entry: all S1-S14 pass; no open Sev-1/Sev-2; penetration test with no open
Critical/High findings.

19. Business Owner responsibilities
These are the items the Business Owner (vendor) must arrange; the development team can't
provide them.
Important correction to a common assumption: API keys and secrets must not be
written into the application code. Anything inside an APK or .exe can be extracted. Keys
are held in the vendor's cloud secret stores and CI; restaurant installations receive their
own scoped credentials from the Control Plane (SEC-002). The Business Owner's job is
to own the accounts and give the development lead controlled access to them.
